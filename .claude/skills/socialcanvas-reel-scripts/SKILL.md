# SocialCanvas Reel Scripts

Use this for any reel work for @SocialCanvasbyRahul: a new script, a rewrite of another creator's script, hook options, a shorter version, a visual hook, a caption, or a Seedance/Omni edit prompt for the reel's footage.

## 1. Channel and audience

- @SocialCanvasbyRahul, Instagram Reels, about 500K followers.
- Core audience: Hindi-speaking graphic designers and creators, many freelancing. Their real pains: clients who want 3 options by tomorrow for 500 rupees, endless revisions, AI output that looks generic, wanting to earn from content without the time to make it.
- Topics: AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT, Higgsfield, Seedance, Google Flow, invideo, Canva), creator workflows, design hacks.
- Default language is Roman-script Hinglish. Write English only when asked. If Rahul pastes Devanagari, return Roman Hinglish unless the output is a video-model prompt (see section 8).

## 2. Voice

- Flowing, not punchy. Long sentences joined with aur, to, kyuki, phir, lekin, jabki. No listicle fragments.
- Talks like a friend: "Bas ye karo", "to dekho main kya karta hoon", "aur bas, video ready".
- Pick tum or aap and keep it the same through the whole script. Follow whichever Rahul used in his latest line.
- Fold features into sentences. Reveals sound like "aur sabse kamaal ki baat ye hai ki...".
- Specific over vague: real numbers, exact tool and setting names ("settings mein Omni 1.1 select karo").
- Never: textbook explanations, "ye teen cheezein change karta hai", hype words like game changer, em dashes, explain-to-a-child framing.

## 3. Structure and length

Default tutorial reel, 30 to 45 seconds (about 75 to 125 words):

1. Hook: one direct line, ideally paired with a visual hook (section 5).
2. Optional series line, very short: "Free mein Claude seekho, part one."
3. Steps: two to four lines, each one an action the viewer can copy. Say where to click and what to paste.
4. Payoff or result line: what they end up with.
5. CTA: one ask only. Either a caps keyword ("comment mein LOGO likho, main DM kar dunga") or "follow karke comment mein apna favourite emoji daal do". Never two options like broadcast channel or comment.

When asked to cut under 30 seconds: keep every action the viewer needs, merge steps, move menu paths to on-screen text, drop the second CTA.

## 4. Hooks: what the channel data says

From the account's own analytics (135 reels, Jan to Oct 2026):

- Announcement hooks fail. "Tum abhi bhi AI se bakwas X bana rahe ho" and "ye secret tool..." openings sat at 80 to 84 percent skip and about 3 seconds average watch (POSTER, Canva Hacks, GPT-6 Astra, the ChatGPT logo-skill reel at 69 percent). Do not lead with a bare tool announcement.
- What wins: the result on screen in the first second, Rahul inside an AI trend, recreating a famous or meme moment, or riding a moment the whole country feels. Winners: 17.2M (national tribute), 5.6M and 2.3M (AI trends), 824K (MEME guide), CCTV 274K.
- Shares drive reach. Winners had far more shares than saves. Build hooks people send to a friend, especially designer-to-designer relatable moments (logo_final_final_v7 folder, "bhaiya logo thoda pop karo" voice note, 14 revisions).
- Best length 35 to 45 seconds. Under 25 kills saves. Trend reels can be about 10 seconds.
- Same-day duplicates lose 90 to 98 percent of reach. Re-cuts go out as Trial Reels.
- If a reel's skip rate is above 60 percent after the first hours, the hook failed, not the content. Fix only the first 3 seconds.

Package the value in the viewer's words, not the tool's. Before writing a hook, ask who is watching and what is already on their mind, then say the result in words they use every day ("Claude ab meri videos edit kar deta hai", not "maine ek MCP banaya"). Keep jargon like MCP, API, agent or model out of the first sentence. See the "Broccoli" entry in `references/reel-breakdowns.md`.

When asked for hook options: give one recommended hook with one line on why, then three or four alternatives. Rahul prefers hooks that are direct and relatable, written as if talking to the audience: "Aapne Instagram pe ye wala viral video to dekha hi hoga, to chalo batata hoon...".

Hooks must sound like Rahul talking to a friend, never like a headline. Open with a friendly address ("yaar", "suno", "dekho", "socho") or a shared moment, speak to "tum" directly, and promise to show them ("chalo dikhata hoon", "batata hoon"). Keep Hook Master's rule that the topic is clear in the first sentence: the friendly word comes first, the topic right after, with no greeting or "welcome back" in between.

## 5. Visual hooks

Rahul shoots himself, then edits with Seedance 2.5 or Google Omni. Good visual hooks are grounded in Indian everyday life, not fictional fantasy:

- A real place transforms on a physical move: shop shutter rolling up, signboard swap on a finger snap, thela banner slap, mithai box lid.
- A topical location as the backdrop: Ramlila, Diwali market, Connaught Place at night.
- The finished result flipping in the first second (brand kit, mockups).
- A designer's real pain shown on screen (client WhatsApp, revision counter).

Avoid copyrighted characters (Doraemon, Marvel, anime): Instagram can mute or remove the reel. Public-domain figures (Mona Lisa) or his own mascot Gappu are safe. Treat devotional figures (Durga Maa, Ram Leela actors) with respect: no glitchy faces, no jokes about them.

Always pair a script with: a text hook in a code block (caps, max two lines, no punctuation), a one-line visual hook, and a shot table when the reel has several clips.

## 6. Rewriting other creators' scripts

- Keep the facts and steps, change the wording, order and hook so it does not read as a copy.
- Swap foreign or branded props for Indian, brand-free ones.
- Drop the original creator's signature moves.
- If the skill, prompt or video is someone else's, never write "maine banaya". Use "ek skill hai jo..." and remind him to credit the creator in the caption.

## 7. Accuracy rules (protect trust)

- Research launches and tools from the official source before writing; use exact feature names, plan limits and regions.
- Never invent numbers, timings or results. Use placeholders like [X views].
- Say "free" only when it is. Flag paid parts: Claude Code needs a paid plan, ChatGPT Work is for paid plans, Higgsfield Katana uses credits, Google Flow and invideo AI run on credits.
- No "within a second" or "10 minutes" claims unless confirmed. Prefer "kuch hi der mein".
- Never promise earnings. Use "kamana chahte ho".
- Not naming an artist does not make using their footage safe. Suggest muted picture-in-picture or skipping it.
- Tell him to test any "phone pe kiya" or "khud install ho gaya" claim before filming it.
- If two reels going out close together share a CTA keyword, flag it so the DM automation sends the right file.
- Menu names change. Name the exact plugin or setting on screen rather than "jo pehle number pe aaye".

## 8. Video-edit prompts (Seedance 2.5, Google Omni)

Before writing an edit prompt for his clip, look at the clip: length, framing, what he does, whether he speaks, what he wears, the light. Then:

- Use his proven format: TASK, (TARGET SCRIPT if he speaks), TIMING if needed, ACTION & MOTION PRESERVATION, then background, relighting, look, audio, AVOID.
- Motion lock line: "Freeze all macro-movements from the original video to match frame-by-frame. The subject's body position, head tilts, shoulder positions, and hand gestures must be perfectly mapped and preserved from the source footage." Never add or change actions.
- Dialogue for Hindi lines: write it in Devanagari with English words in English. Add a timing table matched to the clip length, and check the line fits the clip.
- Relighting is what makes it look real. Describe light by direction and falloff on the face (warm key from the left, cool or stage fill from the right, deep shadow side, rim light), plus contact shadows and matched noise and exposure. Add AVOID: face brighter than background, daylight face, cutout edges, halo.
- Wardrobe swaps (shorts to jeans) and prop meaning ("the black object in his hand is his phone, its screen glows on his face") must be stated explicitly.
- Phone screens: leave them blank or glowing and composite real UI in the edit; AI text on screens comes out garbled.
- Tell him to trim clips (walk-ins, hand covering the lens) before uploading.

## 9. Captions

One line, keyword CTA first, before the "more" cut-off. Credit any creator whose work is used. Three to five hashtags. Example:

Comment "LOGO" and I'll send you the skill and the full workflow in your DM

## 10. Output format

- Script lines only, one sentence per line, blank line between. No timestamps or brackets inside the script.
- After a separator: text hook, visual hook or shot table, and only the notes that change what he shoots or says.
- Keep notes short. No recap of changes unless something factual changed.

## 11. How Rahul iterates

- Short directions: "shorten it", "more direct", "make it viral", "in English", "this is not required", "looks like a copy". Apply fully and return the updated script without long explanations.
- "Change the hook" means change only the hook unless the rest no longer fits.
- If he rewrites a line himself, keep his wording; only fix spelling or the tum/aap mismatch.
- If he says ideas are too fictional or too AI-like, ground them in real Indian places, objects and designer pains, and check them against his analytics.

Read it when writing hooks, when Rahul mentions one of those reels, or when he asks for a relatable "pain first" angle. Add a new entry there whenever a reel is analysed.

## 13. Hook Master (hook diagnosis and rewrites)

`references/hook-master/` holds Hook Master v1.1.0 by Liam Johnston (MIT, see its LICENSE and `hook-master/CREDITS.md`). It is nine small skills that check and fix the first seconds of a reel. They are not installed as separate skills; read the files directly.

When to use it:
- Rahul asks to fix, check or rank a hook, or a reel's skip rate is above 60 percent (section 4).
- Before handing over any new script, run its hook through `four-failures-check` and `viewer-panel` silently.

How to run it: read `references/hook-master/hook-master/SKILL.md` first. It routes to the other eight. When it says to load a stage skill, read `references/hook-master/<stage>/SKILL.md` (the stages are siblings in that folder).

This channel's rules win over Hook Master's where they differ:
- **Language and voice.** All spoken lines and on-screen text in Roman Hinglish and Rahul's voice (sections 1 and 2), not English.
- **Output.** For a full script, keep the section 10 format and add the hook check as short notes after the separator. Use Hook Master's card only when Rahul asks to fix or rank an existing hook.
- **Viewer panel for this audience.** Swap in: the Hindi-speaking doom-scroller, the beginner who has never opened Claude, the working freelance designer, the sceptic who has seen every "secret AI tool" reel, the student who wants to earn, and the rival Hindi tech creator. Weight the doom-scroller and the freelance designer most.
- **Visual first.** Hook Master's `visual-match` agrees with section 5. Ground the first shot in Indian everyday life and Rahul's own footage.
- **Proof and tests.** Use only Rahul's real analytics. For "Test it", compare against this channel's numbers (median skip about 59 percent; median views by skip band in `references/reel-breakdowns.md`) and use Trial Reels for the A/B test.
- **CTA.** One keyword CTA at the end (section 3). Hook Master's "cut follow asks before the payoff" still applies.
- **Viral scout.** Use the entries in `references/reel-breakdowns.md` as the pasted examples. Never claim a trend without them.
- Hook Master's honesty rules (no invented stats, no view promises, facts kept at their true size, no dashes) match section 7. Follow both.
